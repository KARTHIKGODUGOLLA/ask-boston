## DOCUMENT 9: NEWSPAPER ARTICLE — THE CHANNEL CURRENT

**The Channel Current — Fort Point's independent weekly**
**July 2, 2026 · By Marguerite Osei**

### Shut Since 2014, Priced at $110 Million: City Moves on the Northern Avenue Bridge

The swing bridge that has rusted over the Fort Point Channel since 1908 — closed to cars in 1997, fenced off from everyone since December 2014 — finally has a price tag and a plan. City officials now put the full rehabilitation cost at $110 million once escalation and contingency are folded in, and a council committee advanced the design money in May.

"Within two years of breaking ground, we expect people walking across," Public Works Commissioner Althea Grady said at a Harborwalk press event Tuesday, gesturing at the padlocked downtown gate behind her.

The plan would bring the span back for pedestrians and cyclists only — no vehicles. That suits most of the neighbors this paper spoke to. "Saturday mornings used to start on the other side of that bridge," said Yolanda Peña, who runs Gull & Anchor Bakery on Sleeper Street. At last month's Civic Roundtable at Meridian Wharf Café, a caterer described her couriers detouring along Seaport Boulevard, and a water-taxi captain described dodging debris near his float.

Not everyone is patient. The city's own 311 logs show a winter and spring of bent fences, dark approach lights, and people climbing onto the closed span. The commissioner's office says interim fencing and debris netting are funded and coming this summer.

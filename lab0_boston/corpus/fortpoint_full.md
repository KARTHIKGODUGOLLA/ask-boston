## DOCUMENT 9: NEWSPAPER ARTICLE — THE CHANNEL CURRENT

**The Channel Current — Fort Point's independent weekly**
**July 2, 2026 · By Marguerite Osei**

### Shut Since 2014, Priced at $110 Million: City Moves on the Northern Avenue Bridge

The swing bridge that has rusted over the Fort Point Channel since 1908 — closed to cars in 1997, fenced off from everyone since December 2014 — finally has a price tag and a plan. City officials now put the full rehabilitation cost at $110 million once escalation and contingency are folded in, and a council committee advanced the design money in May.

"Within two years of breaking ground, we expect people walking across," Public Works Commissioner Althea Grady said at a Harborwalk press event Tuesday, gesturing at the padlocked downtown gate behind her.

The plan would bring the span back for pedestrians and cyclists only — no vehicles. That suits most of the neighbors this paper spoke to. "Saturday mornings used to start on the other side of that bridge," said Yolanda Peña, who runs Gull & Anchor Bakery on Sleeper Street. At last month's Civic Roundtable at Meridian Wharf Café, a caterer described her couriers detouring along Seaport Boulevard, and a water-taxi captain described dodging debris near his float.

Not everyone is patient. The city's own 311 logs show a winter and spring of bent fences, dark approach lights, and people climbing onto the closed span. The commissioner's office says interim fencing and debris netting are funded and coming this summer.

## DOCUMENT 10: ENGINEERING CONDITION ASSESSMENT (EXCERPT)

**Northern Avenue Bridge — Condition Assessment and Reopening Feasibility Study**
**Prepared for:** City of Boston, Public Works Department
**Prepared by:** Harborline Structural Group · Principal-in-charge: Deirdre Fitzgerald, PE (MA #48291)
**Submitted:** May 11, 2026

**1. Structure.** The Northern Avenue Bridge is a 1908 rim-bearing swing bridge over the Fort Point Channel: three riveted Pratt through-trusses on a central turntable pier, with a timber fender system protecting the pier and approaches.

**2. Service history.** The structure was closed to vehicular traffic in 1997 and closed to all users in December 2014. It has remained fenced at both approaches since.

**3. Findings.**
- Swing-span superstructure condition rating: **2 (critical)** on the NBI 0–9 scale.
- Section loss of up to 60 percent in floor-beam bottom flanges over the channel spans.
- Fatigue cracking at riveted lower-chord connections at six locations.
- Scour to a depth of 1.4 meters at the turntable pier; fender timbers are actively shedding debris into the channel.
- Perimeter fencing breached at both approaches; field crews observed evidence of recurring unauthorized access on the swing span.

**4. Recommendation.** Rehabilitate the span as a pedestrian- and cyclist-only crossing. Rehabilitation cost is estimated at $95 million in 2024 dollars; construction duration is approximately 30 months from notice to proceed. Interim measures: rebuild both approach gates and install debris netting over the fender system ahead of construction.

## DOCUMENT 11: PERSONAL DIARY ENTRY

**From the notebook of Yolanda Peña — June 14, 2026**

Wednesday at the Roundtable, Camila raised her hand before I did. Twice now — first in front of the councilors at City Hall, then at Priya's café — my girl standing up to ask about benches and shade like she owns the deck already. She has been like this since she started the co-op at the engineering firm; there are bridge spreadsheets on my kitchen table next to the flour.

I keep the first dollar from opening day in 2011 taped inside the register, and next to it now the inspector's code sheet from January. Those three days closed cost us more than the fix itself. The walk-in behaves; I check it twice a night anyway.

Aurelio would have teased me for worrying. I still reach for the phone to call him some mornings. Since he died I do the worrying for two, and the hoping for two as well. If they truly reopen the bridge, the downtown regulars come back on foot, Saturdays fill again, and Camila can point at the railing and say her firm drew it.
